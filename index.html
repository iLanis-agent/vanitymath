Vanity math

A bathroom redo ends up a vanity two centimeters too wide for the wall, twin bowls squeezed onto a single-bowl top, or a faucet whose holes do not match the sink. Vanity math turns the wall into the widest standard vanity that fits past the labeled fillers, turns that vanity into the bowl count and the counter left over, and turns the drill spacing into the faucet family. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Vanity plan - the wall past the labeled fillers becomes the widest standard vanity and the room left over.
  • Sink plan - the vanity width becomes the bowl count and the counter left over.
  • Faucet plan - the drill spacing becomes the handle count and the faucet family.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a 2.5 cm filler strip per side, standard widths of 60, 75, 90, 120, 150 and 180 cm, a 45 cm bowl with a 30 cm bridge for a twin pair, and drill families of single-hole up to 5 cm, 10 cm centerset and 20 cm widespread - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real vanities follow the maker's spec sheet, the room's plumbing and the local code. This is planning arithmetic, not an installation design: measure the real wall and let the spec sheet govern.
